# SR-003: EARS review of the #6 semantic module contract spec

## Summary

Every SHALL-bearing statement of issue #6 (StR-001 Stakeholder Need; the
Description, Behavior and Constraint cells of FR-001 through FR-005; the
NFR-001 Statement) plus the eight unmodalled rows of the FR-004 per-type table
were read for EARS pattern, single `shall`, named subject and concrete
response. Quire 0.31.0 (engine 0.46.0) reports 21/21 documents grammar-clean
with zero `[ears:*]` findings, both with `--summary` and with `--strict`
(exit 0): no `non-singular`, `vague-response`, `missing-subject`,
`non-canonical-trigger` or `unclassifiable` statement, and every unwanted
condition that is stated uses `If … then … SHALL`. This spec has already
absorbed the sibling `agent-ix/spec-objects-business#4` EARS dispositions —
the FR-002 check trigger is `If … then`, "stale" is defined, `scripts/stage-npm.mjs`
is named with FR-002-AC-7 behind it, the FR-004 table opens with
"Each model SHALL enforce its row", and NFR-001's quantifier is narrowed to
the population it measures.

The fifteen findings below are what the grammar cannot see. Six mediums:
obligations placed on documents rather than actors (StR-001, FR-001), a
Behavior bullet with no modal that carries a neighbour's behaviour and a
verification decision (FR-003), two statements whose subject is this
specification or its test suite rather than the system (FR-004), three
obligations hidden in a `so that` rationale clause (FR-004), a requirement
whose response is a three-step human procedure (FR-002), and one NFR
statement carrying two obligations (NFR-001). Nine lows are wording: `Where …`
state clauses, `When …` used for conditions, non-actor subjects, compound
Descriptions, obligations on external systems, and positional cross-references.
FR-001 predates this issue and is recorded for completeness.

## Verdict

Grammar: **pass** — the engine is clean in both modes and no finding here is
one the engine could have raised. Semantics: **conditional** — no high; the
six mediums each leave an obligation without a nameable actor, without a
modal, or without an atomic statement to trace a criterion to, and should be
worded before `spec-to-plan` tasks them. The nine lows are recorded without a
change proposal beyond wording.

No spec artifact was edited by this review; every disposition below is a
proposal.

## Findings

| FND-300 | medium | StR-001's Stakeholder Need puts the obligation on the documents — "operational specs **shall** yield extractable graph entities" — and specs do not yield; the module's manifest and schemas make the entities extractable and Quire's extractor produces them. The modal is also authored as lowercase bold `**shall**` while every FR uses `SHALL`, so one requirement class reads in a different register than the rest of the spec. StR-001-VC-3 then packs two obligations into one criterion (each type carries one typed structural contract; the downstream frontends can consume it read-only *and* tell a standing definition from an observed execution without reading prose), so a partial satisfaction has no row to land on. Restate as "The module SHALL make … extractable" and split VC-3. | StR-001 Stakeholder Need, StR-001-VC-3 | wrong-requirement |
| FND-301 | medium | FR-001 (pre-dates this issue) uses the generic subject "The system SHALL publish …" in a module repository where "the system" could be this package, the manifest, or filament-core, and packs two obligations into one `shall`: conformance to FR-035 v1.0.0 and idempotent activation. Its Behavior then states "Re-activation SHALL be a no-op", whose subject is a nominalised event and whose obligation belongs to filament-core, a system this module does not build. Name the module as subject, let Behavior carry the two obligations separately, and restate idempotency as "When the manifest is re-activated with an unchanged content hash, filament-core SHALL register no change" or as a stated dependency assumption on FR-026. | FR-001 Description, FR-001 Behavior | wrong-requirement |
| FND-302 | medium | FR-003 Behavior carries one bullet with no modal at all — "Measured against quire 0.46.0: a refused schema drops that object type alone, while a manifest key the loader cannot parse … drops every object type … Both refusals are silent … the naming half of FR-003-AC-6 is blocked on them and is verified as an explicit expected failure rather than dropped." Three different things sit in a normative list: an observation about a neighbour's current behaviour, two engine defects, and a verification decision. Nothing in it obliges this module to anything, yet FR-003-AC-6 states the blocked half ("refused by Quire's loader naming `foo`") as a plain criterion with no marker, so a reader who takes the criteria as the contract reads a criterion the Behavior says cannot pass. Move the measurement and the defect references to Rationale or Verification, and mark AC-6's naming half as the expected failure the bullet already declares it to be. | FR-003 Behavior (final measurement bullet), FR-003-AC-6 | wrong-requirement |
| FND-303 | medium | Two FR-004 statements take the specification or its test suite as subject rather than the system: "This specification SHALL NOT claim that purity is enforced over field *names*" makes a document the actor and a *claim* the response, which no test can discharge; and "The test suite SHALL verify every criterion over a key the extractor does not populate against a hand-built JSON record rather than an extracted one, naming that limitation in the test itself" is a matrix rule authored as a functional requirement. Both encode real decisions worth keeping — restate the first as the limit it records ("the schemas SHALL NOT be relied on to refuse a field by name; the residual is a review obligation") and move the second to `spec/tests.md` as a matrix rule, where a row can carry it. | FR-004 Behavior (purity-claim bullet, test-suite bullet) | wrong-requirement |
| FND-304 | medium | FR-004's Description states one `SHALL` (declare one model per type whose schema validates the record with type-specific rules) and then hides three further obligations in its `so that` clause: no type is a placeholder; a standing definition is refused where an observed execution is required *and the reverse*; operational evidence is referenced through exactly one key on exactly one type. Two of the three are the ticket's own acceptance criteria and each has a criterion elsewhere (AC-1, AC-11, CON-3), but the middle one is asserted in a direction the schemas do not hold — a `Deployment` record with an identity field and one `Timestamp` row is shape-identical to an observed execution and validates, which the failure-domain review carries as FND-200/FND-202. Reduce the Description to its one obligation and state the bidirectional refusal as a Behavior bullet with its own criterion, so a claim the schema layer cannot make is not carried in a rationale clause. | FR-004 Description, FR-004-AC-1, FR-004-AC-11, FR-004-CON-3; failure-domain FND-200, FND-202 | wrong-requirement |
| FND-305 | medium | FR-002's bump rule is authored as "If the manifest `version` changes, then the bump procedure SHALL be: edit the `@jsonSchema` base … and the manifest `version` in the same commit, run `make schemas`, and commit the re-emitted schemas, the rewritten `$id` and `$ref` values, the regenerated `data_schema.digest` values and `toolchain.json` together; a commit that carries one half of the pair is refused by `make schemas-check`." The subject is a procedure, the response is three human steps, and the one machine-checkable obligation — the refusal — is stated as a fact about a tool rather than as a requirement. FR-002-AC-8 and FR-002-CON-5 already carry the enforceable half. Restate as "`make schemas-check` SHALL exit non-zero if the `@jsonSchema` base version and the manifest `version` differ" (the generator rule two bullets above already says this) and move the editing steps to Rationale or a CONTRIBUTING note. | FR-002 Behavior (bump-procedure bullet), FR-002-CON-5, FR-002-AC-8 | wrong-requirement |
| FND-306 | medium | NFR-001's second Statement paragraph packs two `SHALL` obligations into one sentence — keep every 0.2.0 locator definition and the `configuration-scope` lint rule unchanged, *and* keep the untyped section bodies those locators yield byte-identical — which are measured by two different metrics (NFR-001-AC-1 and NFR-001-AC-4) and can fail independently. The first paragraph likewise embeds its population definition inside the obligation ("— the eight skeletons as they stood at manifest version 0.2.0, which is the population this NFR measures —"), so the statement a reader must satisfy and the scope note a reviewer must check are one sentence. Split into three statements, one per obligation, with the population note moved to Scope. | NFR-001 Statement, NFR-001-AC-1, NFR-001-AC-4 | wrong-requirement |
| FND-307 | low | Non-canonical state and condition keywords the grammar does not flag. `Where …` opens five state clauses (FR-003 "Where an object type gains a locator after 0.2.0 …"; FR-004 "Where a consumer needs a migration's execution state …" and "Where a key is declared but the current extractor does not populate it …"; FR-005 "Where a typed section and a kernel section describe the same declarations …" and "Where the kernel form is the authority, no test SHALL assert agreement …") — all are continuous states, so `While …` is the canonical form. Two `When …` clauses are conditions rather than events: FR-002 "When no `$id` or `$ref` is relative, the generator SHALL record the normalization as `applied: false`" is the complement of the `If … then` rewrite branch above it, and FR-003 "When the install has completed, `quoin module` SHALL list `spec-objects-operational`" is a post-state (`While the module is installed, …`). | FR-002, FR-003, FR-004, FR-005 | wrong-requirement |
| FND-308 | low | Non-actor subjects, a cluster the engine accepts because a subject is present. Build targets act ("`make schemas` SHALL run …", "`make schemas-check` SHALL run …", "`make lint` SHALL run `make schemas-check`"); artifacts act ("The repository SHALL mark `*.json` and `*.tsp` as `eol=lf`", "The Python package SHALL include … in the wheel and sdist", "The manifest SHALL gain a `required: false` `section_body` locator …", "Each skeleton … SHALL author `## Properties` …" — a document does not author); a nominalisation acts ("Emission SHALL be deterministic", FR-002-CON-3); and documents of this spec act ("No acceptance criterion, test, or fixture SHALL hard-code the version segment"). Each reads clearly and each has a criterion, so this is register rather than ambiguity; name the generator, the packaging step, the module or the author where one restatement is cheap. | FR-002 Behavior, FR-002-CON-3, FR-005 Behavior | wrong-requirement |
| FND-309 | low | Compound Descriptions the engine reads as one `SHALL`. FR-002 packs emitter, source, toolchain pin, output directory and a drift-fails-the-build clause; FR-003 packs the `semantic` block, reference-form `data_schema`, the version, and a trailing `while every existing extraction locator, lint rule, and lexicon entry keeps its meaning` that is a compatibility obligation restated in Behavior and owned by NFR-001; FR-005 packs the FR-071/FR-072 authoring forms, validation through Quire, and negative fixtures whose subject is different from the skeletons'. Behavior atomises all three, so traceability holds; reducing each Description to one summary obligation would remove the duplicate statement of NFR-001's rule in FR-003. | FR-002, FR-003, FR-005 Descriptions | wrong-requirement |
| FND-310 | low | Obligations on systems this module does not build, and one on a person. FR-003 "When the install has completed, `quoin module` SHALL list `spec-objects-operational`" and FR-003-CON-1's Quoin half are Quoin's behaviour (CON-1 already says so and scopes its own evidence, which is the pattern the others should follow); FR-004's "… reported by the extractor as `semantic.unresolved-type`" is quire-rs FR-070's behaviour, which FR-004-AC-12 nonetheless asserts; and FR-003's "If Quoin or Quire rejects the manifest, then this module SHALL correct its own manifest or schemas rather than relax the contract keys …" is a maintainer process rule inside an FR, verifiable only by review. Restate each as this module's obligation or as a stated dependency assumption, and move the corrective rule to a constraint with `Inspection` or to an ADR. | FR-003 Behavior, FR-003-CON-1, FR-004 Behavior, FR-004-AC-12 | wrong-requirement |
| FND-311 | low | FR-004's Behavior opens with a definitions paragraph that carries no modal (identity field, occurrence field, measured field — the three readings the whole per-type table depends on) and buries one obligation inside it in the passive with no actor: "a release that renders `identity: false`, namespaces kernel scalars, or moves `unit` off `TypeRef` is a breaking change to these schemas and SHALL be handled by a manifest version bump, not by widening a rule." Who bumps, and what refuses the widening, are unstated. Lift the three definitions into a `## Definitions`-style block or the spec lexicon, and restate the bump obligation with the module as subject beside FR-002-CON-5, which already owns atomic bumps. | FR-004 Behavior (definitions paragraph) | wrong-requirement |
| FND-312 | low | Rationale carried inside the requirement sentence, so the statement a criterion traces to is longer than the obligation.
